# Physiology Rules — Coaching Knowledge Base (10K)

> Calibration file. Maximum density, no filler. Each section: principles → constants → hard rules.
> Every session the coach prescribes must trace back to a line in this file.

## 1. Performance Model — Joyner & Coyle (2008)

**Principle.** Endurance performance is determined by three variables, and only three:

| # | Determinant | What it is | What develops it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **VO2max** | Ceiling of oxygen delivery (ml/kg/min) | Intervals ≥ 90 % HRmax (Z5) |
| 2 | **Fractional utilisation** | % of VO2max sustainable over the distance (threshold / vRCP) | Threshold work in Z4, aerobic volume in Z2 |
| 3 | **Running economy (RE)** | Energy cost, ml O2/kg/km | Heavy strength, plyometrics, volume, technique |

**Conceptual equation:**

```
Sustainable velocity ≈ (VO2max × %VO2max at threshold) / Energy cost
```

**Hard rules**
- The three levers are **partly independent** → a plan that trains only one is incomplete.
- Two runners with identical VO2max can differ by > 10 % over 10K through running economy alone.
- **Adaptation timelines:**
  - VO2max: fast responder, plateaus in **6 to 12 weeks** of intense work. Detrains quickly.
  - Threshold / fractional utilisation: improves over **months**, tightly coupled to accumulated volume.
  - Running economy: improves over **years**. The only lever with no near-term ceiling.
- **Priority by level:**
  - Beginner / intermediate (VO2max < 50-55, volume < 30 km/wk) → **VO2max is the dominant lever**, fastest returns.
  - Trained (volume > 50 km/wk, several seasons) → **threshold + economy**; VO2max barely moves.

## 2. Intensity Distribution — Seiler & Kjerland (2006), Seiler & Tønnessen (2009), Stöggl & Sperlich (2014)

**Three-zone physiological model** (not to be confused with the five training zones in `pacing_and_zones.md`):

| Polarised zone | Ventilatory landmark | Lactate | Maps to Z1-Z5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **POL-1** | < VT1 | < 2 mmol/L | Z1 + Z2 |
| **POL-2** | VT1 → VT2 | 2-4 mmol/L | Z3 + low Z4 — the "grey zone" |
| **POL-3** | > VT2 (RCP) | > 4 mmol/L | high Z4 + Z5 |

**Working constant: 80 / 20**

```
POL-1 : 75-85 %   ← target 80 %
POL-2 :  0-10 %   ← target  0-5 %, minimise outside a dedicated block
POL-3 : 15-20 %   ← target 20 %
```

**Hard rules**
- **Unit of account:** Seiler counts **sessions** and **time**. Volume in km is an acceptable proxy but **understates** the easy share (more km/h are covered in Z5). *When in doubt, count time.*
- **The number one error is drifting into POL-2.** An easy run held at VT1 costs the fatigue of a threshold session without producing the stimulus. Easy must feel **uncomfortably slow**.
- Stöggl & Sperlich (2014): over 9 weeks, polarised beat threshold-only, high-volume and HIIT-only on **VO2max, time to exhaustion and peak velocity**.
- POL-2 is not forbidden: it is marathon pace and long tempo blocks. It is **programmed**, never **drifted into**.
- Practical corollary: **the more intensity you want, the more easy volume you need to absorb it.** You do not add a third hard session on 25 km/week.

## 3. 10K-Specific Target — vRCP (Lourenço et al.)

**Principle.** The **RCP** (Respiratory Compensation Point, ≈ VT2 / upper threshold) and its associated velocity **vRCP** predict 10K performance **better than VO2max alone** among runners of similar level.

**Constants**

```
vRCP       ≈ 85-92 % VO2max
vRCP       ≈ 82-90 % vVO2max (MAS)
10K pace   ≈ 95-100 % vRCP      (the 10K is run AT vRCP or just below)
10K pace   ≈ 80-92 % MAS        (see pacing_and_zones.md §4 for the exact coefficient)
```

**Hard rules**
- **VO2max raises the ceiling; vRCP moves the runner closer to it.** The 10K is won on vRCP.
- Season architecture: **VO2max block first** (raise the ceiling) → **vRCP block next** (specificity), the final 4-6 weeks before the goal race.
- A session at vRCP is the **most specific session** for the 10K: it is literally race pace.
- If target 10K pace exceeds estimated vRCP, the goal is unrealistic → recalibrate, do not encourage.
- Most reliable progress marker outside a test: **the same 4 × 8 min block held at the same HR, faster.**

## 4. Running Economy — Heavy Strength & Plyometrics — Balsalobre-Fernández et al. (2016), Blagrove et al. (2018)

**Principle.** Heavy resistance and plyometric training improve running economy by **2 to 8 %**, **without** raising VO2max and **without** meaningful hypertrophy.

**Mechanism:** ↑ musculotendinous stiffness → ↑ elastic energy return (Achilles tendon, plantar aponeurosis) → ↓ ground contact time → ↓ energy cost. Plus ↑ motor unit recruitment, ↑ intermuscular coordination, ↑ rate of force development (RFD).

**Validated protocol — non-negotiable if you want the effect**

```
Frequency  : 2 to 3 sessions / week
Duration   : ≥ 8 weeks   (effects rarely appear before 6-8 weeks)
Load       : HEAVY, ≥ 80 % 1RM   (typically 85-90 %)
Volume     : 2-4 sets × 3-6 reps
Intent     : EXPLOSIVE concentric, controlled eccentric
Rest       : 2-3 min between sets (full recovery — this is not conditioning)
+ Plyo     : 60-100 ground contacts / session (bounds, jumps, drop jumps, hops)
+ Running  : short hills 8-12 s, bounding, running drills
```

**Hard rules**
- **The 3 × 15 "strength endurance" format does not work** for running economy. Heavy or nothing.
- Core lifts: squat / leg press, deadlift or hip thrust, lunges, **standing AND bent-knee calf raises** (the soleus governs Achilles stiffness — never skip it).
- **Placement:** same day as a hard run (run first, lift ≥ 3 h later if possible), or on a dedicated day. **Never within 24 h before a key quality session.**
- Plyometrics is impact load: progress cautiously, soft surface, never under neuromuscular fatigue.
- Stop heavy lifting **7 to 10 days** before the goal race; keep only a few bounds and strides.
- Contraindication: do not start a heavy strength block at the same time as a volume increase.

## 5. Key Session Catalogue

### 5.1 VO2max — 4 × 4 min (Helgerud et al., 2007)

```
4 × 4 min at 90-95 % HRmax   (≈ 95-100 % MAS)
Recovery: 3 min ACTIVE at ~70 % HRmax
Warm-up: 15 min Z1-Z2 + 3-4 strides
```
- **Stimulus:** maximal cardiac output, stroke volume, mitochondrial density, oxidative enzymes.
- **Reference result:** +5 to +7 % VO2max in 8 weeks, superior to isocaloric moderate training.
- **Dose:** 16 min at target intensity. **1 × / week maximum** during a VO2max block.
- **Why 4 min:** the minimum duration to reach and then hold VO2max; below 3 min the O2 kinetics never plateau.
- **Why active recovery:** keeps HR elevated → VO2max is re-reached faster on the next rep → more cumulative time ≥ 90 % VO2max.

### 5.2 Short-Interval VO2max — 30-30 (Billat)

```
30 s at 100 % MAS / 30 s at 50 % MAS
Sets of 8 to 12 reps, 2 to 3 sets, 4 min between sets
```
- **Stimulus:** maximises time spent ≥ 90 % VO2max at **low lactate cost** (myoglobin re-oxygenation during the slow 30 s limits accumulation).
- **Use:** return to training, fatigued runner, or extra VO2max dose that is better tolerated than 4 × 4.
- **Pace-driven, not HR-driven:** over 30 s, HR lags (60-120 s inertia).

### 5.3 Threshold / vRCP — Tempo Blocks

| Format | Content | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **A — continuous** | 20-30 min at threshold pace (≈ 10K pace + 10-15 s/km) | Threshold base, low damage |
| **B — blocks** | 3-4 × 8-10 min, 2 min jog recovery | More volume at intensity, better quality |
| **C — 10K specific** | 5-6 × 1000 m at **exact 10K pace**, 1:00-1:30 recovery | Specific block, 4-6 weeks out |
| **D — progressive** | 40 min: 20 min Z2 → 15 min Z3 → 5 min Z4 | Transition, pacing rehearsal |

- **Stimulus:** lactate clearance (MCT1/MCT4 transporters), MLSS shift, conversion of IIx → IIa oxidative fibres.
- **Dose:** 1 to 2 × / week, **20 to 40 min of work**.
- **Rule:** threshold must stay "comfortably hard". If the last rep degrades by > 5 s/km the pace was too fast — that is a dosing error, not toughness.

### 5.4 Easy Running — Capillarisation

```
60 to 80 % of weekly volume, in Z1-Z2, STRICTLY below VT1
Marker: HR < 75-80 % HRmax, able to speak in full sentences
```
- **Stimulus:** capillary density, plasma volume, fat oxidation, cardiac remodelling (↑ stroke volume), mitochondrial density in type I fibres, tendon robustness.
- **Long run:** 1 × / week, **25 to 30 % of weekly volume**, capped at 2 h for a 10K goal.
- **Rule:** easy volume is what licenses the intensity. Easy running is not padding.

### 5.5 Strides

```
6 to 8 × 20 s fast and RELAXED (≈ 1500 m pace), full walking recovery
2 × / week, at the end of an easy run
```
- **Stimulus:** neuromuscular, stride frequency, technique. **Zero fatigue cost.**
- **Does not count** toward the 20 % hard share.

## 6. 10K Pacing

**Principle. Even pacing is optimal over 10K.** Any variation costs: a fast start creates an O2 deficit and early acidosis that are never repaid.

**Constants**

```
Target pace variation : < 2 % around mean pace
Start                 : NEVER more than +2 % velocity over the first 2 km
Recommended profile   : km 1-2  → target pace + 3 to 5 s/km
                        km 3-8  → target pace
                        km 9-10 → target pace − 3 to 5 s/km  (slight negative split)
```

**Hard rules**
- A slight negative split outperforms a positive split and is far more robust psychologically.
- **HR is unusable over the first 3 km** (2-3 min cardiac inertia) → pace-driven.
- Never publish split times without checking that they sum to the target time.
- A goal whose pace exceeds estimated vRCP is not a goal: it is a scheduled blow-up at km 7.

## 7. Cross-Cutting Hard Rules

| # | Rule | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **80/20** on weekly volume, no exceptions | Seiler — see §2 |
| 2 | **48 h minimum** between two hard sessions | Glycogen resynthesis + neuromuscular recovery |
| 3 | **+10 % / week maximum** volume growth | Connective tissue (tendon, bone) adapts far slower than the cardiovascular system |
| 4 | Down week **−30 to −40 %** every **3 to 4 weeks** | Supercompensation, overreaching prevention |
| 5 | **One quality session per day** | Competing stimuli interfere |
| 6 | **Volume before intensity**; duration before speed | Build the container before the contents |
| 7 | **Never** introduce a new intensity AND a volume increase in the same week | One variable at a time |
| 8 | **2 hard sessions / week = norm; 3 = absolute ceiling** (and only above 50 km/wk) | See rule 1 |
| 9 | Heavy strength: **≥ 8 weeks** or do not start | Adaptation timeline — see §4 |
| 10 | **Red flag:** resting HR +7 bpm for 2 days, or HR +8-10 bpm above normal at a known pace → session eased or cancelled | Markers of autonomic fatigue |
| 11 | Any pain that **alters running form** → stop, do not adjust pace | Compensation causes the secondary injury |
| 12 | 10K taper: **−40 to −50 % volume over 7-10 days, intensity maintained** | Keep the stimulus, shed the fatigue |
